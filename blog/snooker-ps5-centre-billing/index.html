How to bill by the hour at a snooker or PS5 centre

By Abhinand · 21 November 2026 · 3 min read

At a busy game centre, most arguments at the counter are about time. The customer says they started at 7:15, the register says 7:00, and the person at the desk has to decide who to believe while four other people wait to pay. The fix is a few clear rules, written down and followed every time, whether you use a notebook or software.

Put the rates on the wall

Customers argue less with a rate card they saw before they played. For each activity, write:

  • The rate and its unit. Per table per hour for snooker and pool. Per console per hour for PS5, and whether a third or fourth controller costs extra. Per car per session for RC tracks.
  • The minimum. Many centres charge a minimum of 30 minutes, so a ten-minute game still pays for half an hour.
  • How time is rounded. For example, "charged in 15-minute blocks after the first hour". Pick one rule and use it for everyone.
  • Peak and off-peak rates, if you have them, with the exact hours.

When the rule is on the wall, the desk points at it instead of arguing.

Start, pause, extend, move

Every session has the same few events. Agree on what each one means:

  • Start: the time is written when the table is handed over, never when the customer comes to pay.
  • Pause: allowed only for a real break (a power cut, a broken cue). Write the pause and resume times.
  • Extend: a fixed-time booking that runs over becomes "extended", charged at the normal rate.
  • Move: when players shift from table 2 to table 4, the time carries over. Nobody should restart the clock.

With a paper register, put each event on its own line with the time. Most disputes come from a single line with two times crossed out.

Snacks and drinks on the same bill

Cold drinks, chips and coffee often go missing from the bill because nobody adds them until the end. Write each item on the table's line when it is handed over. At closing, the table's bill is the time plus the items, and the customer sees both.

When the floor is full

On weekend evenings every table may be busy. Keep a waiting list with the customer's name, phone number and the activity they want. Call them in order when something frees up. A customer who sees their name on a list waits. A customer who only heard "maybe twenty minutes" usually leaves.

Close the cash every shift

At the end of each shift, count the cash and add up card and online payments, then compare both with what the register says was billed. Do it with the person handing over and the person taking over. A small difference found the same night has an explanation. The same difference found at the end of the month does not.

Where software helps

A notebook can do everything above if the staff are careful. Software removes the parts that depend on memory:

  • each table, console and track shows its own running timer and its cost so far
  • pauses, extensions and moves are recorded with the time
  • snacks are added to the table while the game is on
  • the bill adds time and items, and takes cash, digital payments or both
  • the shift close shows what each staff member billed
