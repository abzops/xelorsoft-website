WhatsApp Business app or WhatsApp API: which one does your business need?

By Abhinand · 21 November 2026 · 4 min read

Two products share the WhatsApp Business name. The WhatsApp Business app is the free app you install on a phone. The WhatsApp Business Platform, usually called the WhatsApp API, is what CRMs, chatbots and booking systems connect to. Most small businesses in Kerala should start with the app. This guide shows when that stops being enough.

What the free app does well

The WhatsApp Business app is free to download and use. It gives you:

  • a business profile with your address, hours and website
  • a catalogue of products or services with prices
  • quick replies, so a saved answer goes out when you type / and a word
  • labels such as "new lead", "paid" or "follow up" to sort chats
  • a greeting message for new customers and an away message outside your hours
  • broadcast lists, which reach customers who have saved your number

For a shop, salon or clinic where one or two people answer messages during the day, this covers most needs.

Where the app runs out

The same few problems show up as messages grow:

  • Nights and Sundays. The away message tells people you are closed. Many of them message a competitor who answers.
  • Several people on one number. Staff pass the phone around, two people answer the same customer, or nobody answers because each thought the other did.
  • Instagram and Facebook in separate places. Leads from Instagram DMs and the Facebook page sit in other apps, and some are never seen.
  • No record of leads. A customer who asked about a price last week is somewhere in the chat list, but nobody knows who to follow up.

When these cost you customers every week, it is time to look at the API.

What the WhatsApp API is

The WhatsApp Business Platform lets software send and receive messages on your business number. There is no app to open: you use it through a tool built on it, such as a shared inbox, a CRM or an AI assistant. With it you can:

  • reply automatically at any hour, with answers based on your own business information
  • let several staff work the same number from their own logins
  • keep leads, orders and follow-ups attached to each customer
  • bring WhatsApp, Instagram and Facebook messages into one inbox, if the tool supports them

There are rules too. When a customer messages you, a 24-hour window opens for free-form replies. Outside that window, a business can only send pre-approved message templates, and Meta reviews those templates.

How API pricing works in India

Meta charges for the API per delivered message. It moved to per-message pricing on 1 July 2025, and from 1 October 2026 it also charges for service replies inside the 24-hour window, after a free allowance of 1,000 service messages per business number each month. Marketing templates cost the most, and utility messages such as order or booking updates cost much less.

Rates change a few times a year, so check Meta's current rate card for India before you budget. The tool you use on top of the API usually charges its own fee as well.

Which one should you choose?

Stay on the app if one or two people answer messages, most come during working hours, and you rarely lose a lead because a reply was late.

Move to an API tool if leads come at night, more than two people need to answer, Instagram and Facebook messages also bring you business, or you want each lead recorded until it becomes a sale.

Keep the cost per lead in mind. If one extra sale a week covers the monthly cost, the API pays for itself.
