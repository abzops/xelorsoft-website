Does your salon need GST? A guide for Kerala salon owners

By Abhinand · 21 November 2026 · 4 min read

Salon owners ask us about GST more than about any software feature. The short answer: most small salons in Kerala do not have to register yet, and a salon that is not registered must not add GST to its bills. The details below cover when that changes.

This is general information, checked in November 2026. GST rules change, so confirm your own case with a tax consultant before you register or change your bills.

When registration becomes compulsory

A business that supplies services must register for GST once its aggregate turnover in a financial year (April to March) crosses ₹20 lakh. Kerala is a normal-category state, so the ₹20 lakh limit applies. The higher ₹40 lakh limit you may have heard about is for businesses that sell only goods, and a salon sells services.

"Aggregate turnover" means everything the salon bills: haircuts, facials, bridal packages, and any shampoo or cosmetics you sell over the counter. If you run two salons under the same PAN, both count together.

A rough monthly guide: ₹20 lakh a year is about ₹1.67 lakh a month. If your monthly billing is getting close to that, start talking to a consultant before you cross it.

The rate on salon services

Hairdressing, beauty treatments, manicure and pedicure, and non-medical spa services are taxed at 18%. On a bill inside Kerala that 18% appears as two parts: 9% CGST and 9% SGST.

So a ₹1,000 haircut becomes ₹1,180 for the customer, or you keep the price at ₹1,000 and the salon's share drops to about ₹847. Decide this before you register, because changing prices overnight is what customers notice.

Below ₹20 lakh: no GST on the bill

If your salon is not registered, you cannot collect GST from customers. A bill that adds "GST 18%" without a GST number on it is collecting tax you are not allowed to collect. Give a plain bill with the service and the amount.

Registering before you have to

A salon can register voluntarily, even below ₹20 lakh. People do it for two reasons:

  • Input tax credit. A registered salon can claim back the GST it paid on products, equipment and rent from GST-registered suppliers, against the GST it collects.
  • Corporate or brand work. Some companies, hotels and wedding planners only work with GST-registered vendors.

The cost is regular returns, a consultant's fee for most owners, and the 18% on every bill from the day you register. For a neighbourhood salon with walk-in customers, waiting until the limit is usually simpler.

The composition scheme

Small service businesses with turnover up to ₹50 lakh can choose the composition scheme. You pay a lower flat rate on turnover and file simpler returns, but you cannot add GST to customer bills and cannot claim input tax credit. Whether it suits you depends on how much you spend on products and how many of your customers need a GST bill. This is the question to take to your consultant.

What a GST bill must show

Once registered, every bill needs:

  • your salon's name, address and GSTIN
  • a bill number in a series and the date
  • the customer's name (and their GSTIN, if they are a registered business)
  • each service with its SAC code (salon services fall under 9997)
  • the amount before tax, then CGST and SGST shown separately
  • the total

Handwritten bill books can carry all this, but every bill then has to be typed into returns later. Billing software does that part as you go.
